# Seed Gap - AI-Synthesized Knowledge Provenance

## Gap Identification

**What gap occurred:** The Seed tracks knowledge source type (intuition, analysis, authority, experiment) but lacks explicit guidance for **AI-synthesized knowledge** — knowledge generated by AI agents through synthesis of multiple sources. As AI-assisted knowledge management grows, an increasing portion of knowledge may be AI-generated rather than human-authored, and this has different provenance implications.

**What rule is missing from the Seed:** A rule addressing how to track, verify, and weight AI-synthesized knowledge differently from human-authored knowledge.

## Evidence

This gap manifests when:
- AI agents synthesize multiple sources into new insights
- Knowledge is generated through AI reasoning rather than direct human authorship
- Questions arise about reproducibility, verification, and trust of AI-generated content
- The provenance chain includes AI as an active participant, not just a tool

## Why This Matters

1. **Different verification needs** — AI can make errors that humans wouldn't (hallucinations, misinterpretation)
2. **Reproducibility** — Human synthesis documents reasoning; AI synthesis may not be reproducible
3. **Attribution** — AI-generated knowledge has unclear authorship and intellectual property status
4. **Confidence calibration** — AI confidence doesn't map to human confidence (AI often overconfident on wrong answers)
5. **Evolving capability** — AI capabilities change rapidly; knowledge age matters more

## Proposed Rule

**Rule:** Track AI synthesis explicitly — add a knowledge source type category for AI-synthesized knowledge with specific verification requirements.

**Why:** Without explicit AI-synthesis tracking, AI agents cannot apply appropriate verification methods. AI-generated knowledge requires different checks than human-authored content (source verification, consistency checks, freshness verification). The existing source-type rule doesn't account for AI as an active synthesizer.

**Test:** Can you categorize knowledge as: (1) human-authored, (2) human-synthesized, (3) AI-assisted (AI as tool), (4) AI-synthesized (AI as active participant)? Does each category have appropriate verification requirements?

## Implementation

Add frontmatter for AI synthesis tracking:
```yaml
synthesis-source: ai-assisted | ai-synthesized | human-authored | human-synthesized
ai-model: gpt-4 | claude-3 | etc.  # which model generated
ai-verification-status: unverified | cross-checked | human-reviewed
ai-generated-date: 2026-03-27
```

Verification requirements by type:
- **AI-assisted:** Human reviewed → treat as human-synthesized
- **AI-synthesized:** Requires cross-source verification OR human review
- **Human-authored:** Standard verification
- **Human-synthesized:** Standard verification (already applies)

## Edge Cases

1. **AI as research assistant** — AI finds and summarizes sources → human synthesizes → human-synthesized
2. **AI as brainstorming partner** — AI generates ideas, human evaluates → mixed
3. **Pure AI generation** — AI creates without human oversight → highest verification burden
4. **AI improvement** — AI improves existing knowledge → track delta and verification

## Hallucination vs Synthesis Distinction (Merged from Redundant Gap)

AI-synthesized knowledge differs from hallucination in an important way:

- **Synthesis:** Combines multiple known sources into new insight — can be traced back to sources
- **Hallucination:** Generates plausible claims with no source grounding — no provenance chain

The Seed identifies ungrounded content through:
- Empty `synthesis-sources:` list — indicates no source backing
- `author-type: ai-assisted` or `ai-synthesized` — marks AI-generated content for scrutiny
- Confidence defaults to `emerging` for AI-autonomous capture
- Verification status field tracks certainty level

When `synthesis-sources:` cannot be populated, this signals the content is ungrounded — no separate groundedness field is needed.
