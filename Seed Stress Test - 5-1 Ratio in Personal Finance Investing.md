# Seed Stress Test: 5:1 Ratio Rule in Personal Finance/Investing

**Domain tested:** Personal Finance and Investing Knowledge Base  
**Rule tested:** "Apply the 5:1 personal-to-general knowledge ratio — capture insights, decisions, and experiences (personal) over restatable facts (general)."  
**Date:** 2026-03-18

## Analysis

### Does this rule make sense for personal finance?

**Partially.** Personal finance sits in an awkward middle ground:
- Some knowledge IS personal (your specific tax situation, your risk tolerance, your goals)
- Some knowledge IS general but essential (tax laws, market mechanics, account types)
- Much knowledge becomes personal through APPLICATION (a general principle applied to your situation)

The rule assumes "personal = original insight, general = easily googlable." In investing, this breaks down.

### Is the rule's Test executable?

**Test:** "Pick 10 recent notes. For each: (1) Could a web search replace this note in <30 seconds? (2) Does it contain original insight, decision, or experience? Ratio should be 5:1 personal to general."

**Problems in investing:**

1. **Market knowledge has expiration:** "The S&P 500 returned 10% historically" is general but needs a date. "The 60/40 portfolio worked in the 2010s" is general but time-specific.

2. **General principles have personal applications:** "Diversification reduces risk" is general knowledge. But "Diversification didn't save me in 2022" is personal experience that's HIGHLY valuable.

3. **Your specific situation IS the personal knowledge:** Your tax bracket, your account types, your family situation - these aren't "insights" but they're what makes general advice applicable.

4. **Failure modes are highly personal:** "Don't time the market" is general advice. But WHY you personally failed to follow it - that's the valuable personal knowledge.

### Edge cases identified

#### Edge Case 1: General Knowledge with Personal Context

**Problem:** "Tax-loss harvesting" is a general concept. A note explaining it is general knowledge. But "I used tax-loss harvesting in 2024 to offset $X in gains" is personal.

The ratio counts these differently, but they're often the SAME note with different framing.

**Resolution:** Notes should be counted as "personal-equivalent" if they include personal APPLICATION of general knowledge, even if the concept itself is general.

#### Edge Case 2: Domain-Specific Reliability Sources

**Problem:** In investing, some sources are universally referenced (SEC, IRS). A note citing "IRS Publication 590-B" is citing a general source, but the INSIGHT about what it means for YOU is personal.

**Resolution:** Allow "source-referenced personal" - notes that cite authoritative general sources but include YOUR interpretation or application.

#### Edge Case 3: Decision Documentation vs. Outcome Documentation

**Problem:** "I decided to allocate 60% stocks/40% bonds" - this is a personal decision. "The 60/40 portfolio returned 8% in 2024" - this is general market data.

But both are valuable. The rule favors decisions over outcomes, but in investing, OUTCOMES teach you what decisions worked.

**Resolution:** Track "decision notes" and "outcome notes" separately. Both count toward personal ratio, but for different reasons.

#### Edge Case 4: Credentialed Personal Knowledge

**Problem:** A CPA's note about tax strategy is "personal" (their insight) but also "expert knowledge" (could help others). How to count?

**Resolution:** If the note includes personal context (what worked for their specific clients, edge cases they've seen), count as personal.

#### Edge Case 5: Framework Adaptation

**Problem:** You take a general framework (e.g., the 4% rule) and adapt it to your situation (you use 3.5% because you're self-employed). Is this personal?

The framework is general. The adaptation is personal. The note likely contains both.

**Resolution:** If a note contains ANY personal adaptation of general knowledge, count as personal-equivalent.

#### Edge Case 6: Timing Knowledge

**Problem:** "I rebalanced annually" - this is a personal decision pattern. "The best time to rebalance is..." - this is general.

Both are useful. The rule penalizes timing knowledge that could be general.

**Resolution:** Personal timing decisions should count as personal even if they could be generalized.

#### Edge Case 7: Network Knowledge

**Problem:** In investing, WHO you know matters. "My financial advisor told me..." - is this personal? It's personal source but general advice.

**Resolution:** Network knowledge (what specific people told you) counts as personal because it's not reproducible from general sources.

#### Edge Case 8: Account-Specific Knowledge

**Problem:** "I keep 6 months expenses in my HYSA" - this is specific to YOUR accounts and YOUR emergency fund size.

This is personal BUT it's also replicable advice (keep 6 months in HYSA). The "personal" value is that YOU implemented it.

**Resolution:** Implementation decisions count as personal - the act of applying knowledge creates personal value even if the knowledge is general.

## Modified Test for Personal Finance/Investing

For personal finance notes:
1. Does this include personal context (your specific situation, your decisions, your outcomes)?
2. Does this include YOUR interpretation of general knowledge?
3. Does this document a decision you made (not just advice)?
4. Does this include an outcome (what happened when you tried something)?

Count as "personal-equivalent" if yes to ANY of these, not just original insights.
