Seed Gap - Education and Pedagogy Knowledge Bases

Does the Seed adequately address the unique needs of education and pedagogy knowledge bases?

The Question

Education knowledge bases serve fundamentally different purposes than typical personal knowledge bases:

  • Teaching others (creating lessons, curricula)
  • Learning oneself (tracking progress, understanding concepts)
  • Professional development (teaching methods, educational theory)

Does the Seed’s rules work for this domain, or are there gaps?

Domain Characteristics

Unique Knowledge Types in Education

  1. Curriculum Structure

    • Scope and sequence
    • Learning objectives
    • Assessment criteria
    • Prerequisite chains (critical!)

  2. Lesson Planning

    • Timing and pacing
    • Materials and resources
    • Differentiation (adapting for different learners)
    • Activity types

  3. Pedagogical Theory

    • Teaching methodologies
    • Learning theories
    • Educational psychology
    • Assessment strategies

  4. Student-facing Content

    • Explanations (need to be clear, accessible)
    • Examples and analogies
    • Practice problems
    • Feedback mechanisms

Stress Test: Applying Seed Rules

Test 1: Atomicity

Rule: Every note must contain exactly one idea, summarizable in one sentence.

Application: Does this work for lesson plans?

  • A lesson typically has: objective, materials, procedure, assessment
  • These could be separate notes OR one lesson plan note
  • The Seed’s procedural content exception (recipes, tutorials) may apply

Question: Should lesson plans be atomic (one lesson = one note) or compound (unit = one note with lessons as sections)?

Edge Case: A 90-minute lesson with multiple activities, examples, and assessments - splitting would lose coherence.

Test 2: 5:1 Personal-to-General Ratio

Rule: Capture insights, decisions, and experiences (personal) over restatable facts (general).

Application: Education knowledge bases often contain:

  • General pedagogical theory (what experts say)
  • Your interpretation/application (personal)
  • Student-specific adaptations (very personal)

Question: Does the 5:1 ratio make sense for a teacher building a methods vault?

Potential Issue: During learning phase (studying educational theory), general knowledge dominates. The Seed’s edge case for “creative skill domains” might apply.

Test 3: Construction Phase Model

Rule: Apply relaxed standards during bootstrap, tighten as vault matures.

Application: Building an education vault often follows known curriculum structures:

  • Grade levels (K-12)
  • Subject areas (math, science, language)
  • Skill progressions (basic → advanced)

Question: Can we use the curriculum structure itself as the construction phase model?

Test 4: Verification

Rule: Track verification ratio - verify at least 50% of captures within 30 days.

Application: Education knowledge is verified through:

  • Student success (test scores, understanding)
  • Classroom implementation (did the lesson work?)
  • Peer feedback

Issue: Verification timeline is different:

  • Immediate: Lesson taught → did it work? (can be same day)
  • Short-term: Unit complete → assessment results (1-2 weeks)
  • Long-term: Course complete → final outcomes (semester)

Question: Should education vaults have domain-specific verification windows?

Test 5: Expertise Level Tagging

Rule: Tag notes with expertise level (beginner/intermediate/advanced).

Application: Multiple expertise dimensions in education:

  • Teacher expertise (novice teacher vs veteran)
  • Student expertise (what they’re learning)
  • Content complexity (what level is this material?)

Issue: Who is the expertise level for? The teacher building the vault, or the students being taught?

Identified Gaps

Gap 1: Dual-Audience Knowledge

The Seed assumes knowledge is for the vault owner. Education vaults often serve:

  • Self (teacher’s professional development)
  • Students (lesson content)
  • Other teachers (sharing resources)

Question: How should notes indicate their intended audience?

Gap 2: Curriculum Structure vs Emergent Structure

The Seed prefers emergent structure (hubs form as topics grow). Curriculum is intentional structure (grade level → subject → unit → lesson).

Question: Should education vaults use curriculum-first organization instead of allowing organic emergence?

Gap 3: Temporal Knowledge Cycles

Education operates on fixed cycles:

  • Semester system
  • Academic years
  • Grade-level progressions

Question: Should education vaults explicitly model these temporal structures?

Gap 4: Assessment Integration

How do assessments fit into the knowledge graph?

  • Tests and quizzes as knowledge?
  • Assessment results as verification?
  • Standards alignment?

Gap 5: Differentiation Knowledge

How to handle multiple versions of content for different learners?

  • Same concept, multiple difficulty levels
  • Same lesson, multiple student profiles
  • Notes vs. variants

Proposed Seed Rules

Rule Proposal 1: Audience Tagging

Rule: For vaults serving multiple audiences, include explicit audience: frontmatter to indicate who the note is for.

Why: Knowledge that helps you teach may not be the same as knowledge you teach. Without audience tagging, AI agents cannot determine appropriate presentation.

Rule Proposal 2: Curriculum-First Organization

Rule: For education vaults with defined curricula, use intentional curriculum structure as the primary organization, with emergent topics as secondary.

Why: Curriculum structure is externally imposed (standards, grade levels). Fighting it creates friction. The Seed’s “explicit completion criteria” is especially important here - what does “complete” mean for a curriculum?

Test: Can you trace: Standard → Grade Level → Subject → Unit → Lesson?

Rule Proposal 3: Verification Windows by Evidence Type

Rule: Education vaults should use evidence-type-specific verification windows:

  • Lesson effectiveness: immediate (same day/week)
  • Unit comprehension: short-term (2-4 weeks)
  • Course outcomes: long-term (semester)
  • Retention: very long-term (years)

Why: Verification in education has different timescales than other domains. A 30-day window misses the reality of educational verification.

Rule Proposal 4: Differentiation Variants

Rule: When the same knowledge needs multiple presentations for different learner levels, use variant notes with explicit base-concept: linking.

Why: A lesson on “fractions” for 3rd grade differs from 6th grade. Both are valid, neither is “more correct.”
