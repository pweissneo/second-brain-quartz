# Distinguishing Craft Criticism from Aesthetic Judgment

In creative domains, evaluative claims fall into two fundamentally different categories: craft criticism (verifiable, objective) and aesthetic judgment (subjective, opinion-based). Understanding this distinction is essential for building useful knowledge bases.

## The Core Distinction

| Type | What It Measures | Verifiable? | Example |
|------|------------------|-------------|---------|
| **Craft Criticism** | Technical execution, skill, adherence to principles | Yes | "This sculpture has anatomical errors" |
| **Aesthetic Judgment** | Beauty, taste, personal preference | No | "This sculpture is beautiful" |

## Why It Matters

1. **Different truth values** — Craft claims can be right or wrong; aesthetic claims cannot
2. **Different resolution paths** — Craft errors can be fixed with technique; aesthetic disagreements are personal
3. **Different transferability** — Craft skills transfer across styles; aesthetic preferences are style-dependent

## How to Apply

### For Note Authors

When writing evaluative content about creative work:

1. **Identify the claim type** — Is this about how something was done (craft) or whether it's good (aesthetic)?
2. **Separate explicitly** — Use clear language to distinguish: "Technically proficient but not to my taste"
3. **Tag appropriately** — Add `claim-type: craft` or `claim-type: aesthetic` frontmatter

### For Note Readers

When reading evaluative content:

1. **Ask: is this verifiable?** — Can you test this claim empirically?
2. **Ask: is this style-dependent?** — Would a different style produce a different judgment?
3. **Separate opinion from fact** — Treat aesthetic judgments as one perspective, not universal truth

## Examples

### Music
- **Craft:** "This performance uses incorrect fingerings for the passage" (verifiable)
- **Aesthetic:** "This performance is more emotionally engaging" (subjective)

### Visual Arts
- **Craft:** "The perspective in this painting is mathematically incorrect" (verifiable)
- **Aesthetic:** "The color palette is more harmonious" (subjective)

### Writing
- **Craft:** "This sentence uses a passive voice" (verifiable)
- **Aesthetic:** "This prose is more lyrical" (subjective)

## Test Criteria

- [ ] Can you identify whether each evaluative claim is craft or aesthetic?
- [ ] Are craft claims supported by measurable criteria?
- [ ] Are aesthetic claims marked as subjective or tagged with consensus level?
- [ ] Do notes acknowledge when a claim spans both categories?
