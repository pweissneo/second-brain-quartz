# Best Practice - Multi-Source Synthesis

> Synthesize across sources, don't depend on one. Your synthesis is the value.

## The Principle

Single-source dependency means relying entirely on one source for a piece of knowledge. Multi-source synthesis means combining multiple sources to create something more valuable than any single source.

## Why Multiple Sources

1. **Reduces risk** — One source can be wrong, outdated, or biased
2. **Adds context** — Different sources highlight different aspects
3. **Creates unique value** — Your synthesis is what can't be found elsewhere

## How to Synthesize

When capturing knowledge:
1. **Consult multiple sources** — At least 2-3 for important knowledge
2. **Compare and contrast** — What's consistent? What's different?
3. **Add your judgment** — Which do you trust and why?
4. **Document your reasoning** — Your synthesis should be explainable

## The Test

For important knowledge, can you:
- Name 2+ sources that inform this?
- Explain how they agree or differ?
- State why you weigh them as you do?

If yes, you have multi-source synthesis.

## When One Source Is Acceptable

- Foundational, well-established knowledge (textbooks, canonical references)
- Your own direct experience and experiments
- Specific technical documentation for a particular tool

## The Value

Your knowledge base should contain YOUR synthesis, not just copied sources. That's what makes it irreplaceable.
